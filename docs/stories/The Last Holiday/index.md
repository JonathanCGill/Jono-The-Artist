      <h1 class="m-display">The Last Holiday</h1>
    </div>
    <p class="m-lead">The Grote Die in Amsterdam Noord, repainted in cadmium and cerulean, with a magpie, a small green guardian and a travelling snail going nowhere.</p>
  </div>

  <figure class="m-plate">
    <div class="m-plate__inner">
      <img loading="eager" decoding="async" src="The%20Last%20Holiday%20-%20Amsterdam.jpg" width="3006" height="2244" alt="The Last Holiday, the Grote Die canal mirroring houses and a spire, a magpie overhead and a snail moored in a small boat">

  <div class="m-caption">
    <span class="m-caption__title">The Last Holiday</span>
    <span>1000 × 750 mm · Acryla Gouache on canvas · 2025 · Original</span>
  </div>

  <div class="m-story-body">
    <figure class="m-ref">
      <img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="Amsterdam%20Noord.jpg" width="3946" height="2886" alt="The Grote Die in Amsterdam Noord in the early morning, the spire of the Sint-Augustinuskerk behind the trees">
      <figcaption>The scene. Grote Die, Amsterdam Noord, early morning.</figcaption>
    </figure>
    <div class="m-prose">
      <p class="m-pull">The photograph gives you the scene. The painting gives you what the scene felt like.</p>
      <p>Early morning, the light doing what it does on Dutch water. The houses sit quietly, the spire of the Sint-Augustinuskerk anchors the skyline, and the canal gives everything back as a perfect mirror. A still, honest moment. And then I painted over it.</p>
      <p>The muted Dutch palette goes. In its place: cadmium reds, cerulean blues, chrome yellows. The houses find their personality. The sky catches fire at the edges.</p>
      <p>The reflection stays. That was non-negotiable. The canal still holds the buildings, but now it holds colour too, the way water looks when you stop trying to paint it accurately and start painting it honestly.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="m-wrap m-details">
    <div class="m-details__grid">
      <div class="m-details__cell">
        <span class="m-kicker">The magpie</span>
        <p>Lands on the Sint-Augustinuskerk, because magpies were everywhere that trip, strutting around like they owned the place. They probably do.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="m-details__cell">
        <span class="m-kicker">The green figure</span>
        <p>In reality, a sign warning of children playing. In the painting, a small guardian at the edge of the frame, directing your eye inward.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="m-details__cell">
        <span class="m-kicker">ES Cargo</span>
        <p>My snail, the travelling snail, moored in the middle of the canal with the whole morning ahead of him and no intention of using it. He turns up in most of my paintings now.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="m-wrap m-closing">
    <p>Poppies flood the foreground because a canal bank without wildflowers felt like a lie. Daisies and sunflowers crowd in beside them. The reeds from the photograph are still there, but now they compete for space with everything else.</p>
    <p class="m-closer">It is not illustration. It is translation.</p>
  </div>
