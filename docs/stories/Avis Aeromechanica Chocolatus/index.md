<div class="story-text" markdown>

The two watercolour blueprints came first. A side profile and a top profile, studies in how a bird might work if it were engineered rather than evolved. Part avian, part mechanical, drawn out in the way you would plan a machine: elevations, cross-sections, annotations that look technical but are not.

The acryla gouache painting took those blueprints and built the thing. A bright orange livery and pink wings replaced the watercolour washes. The bird plane gained weight and presence. What had been a diagram became an object, sitting in space, casting shadow.

The name says what it is. Avis, bird. Aeromechanica, a machine that flies. Chocolatus, because chocolate is its fuel. The orange livery is there because orange is a bright, cheerful colour, and a bird plane that runs on chocolate ought to look the part.

The integration drawings came later. FlightGPT 0.4 and FlightGPT 1.0 are two passes at the same problem: how the bird's brain and the AI that flies the aircraft talk to each other. The earlier version is rougher, more tentative, working out where emotion ends and instrumentation begins. The later version is tidier, more committed, with the wiring laid out and the channels named. Putting them side by side shows the thinking move from sketch to schematic, and the question itself sharpen. How do feeling and data meet, and which one decides.
